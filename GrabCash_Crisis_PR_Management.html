Response Framework: The FACT Method

F — Fact-Check STEP 1

Before responding to any attack, verify every claim. Is any part of the negative content true? If yes, acknowledge and address that part honestly. If entirely false, document the falsehood with evidence.

A — Assess Impact STEP 2

How far has this spread? Is it reaching our users, admins, or brand partners? Or is it contained to the competitor's bubble? Not every attack needs a public response — sometimes ignoring denies oxygen.

C — Counter-Narrative STEP 3

Prepare factual counter-narrative. Use data, testimonials, and verifiable claims. Deploy through organic channels — admin network, loyal users, supportive creators — not paid amplification.

T — Take the High Road STEP 4

Never engage in public mud-slinging. Never name the competitor in your response. Focus on your strengths and your users' positive experiences. The goal is to make the attack look petty while you look professional.

What NOT to Do:

  • Do NOT publicly accuse the competitor of orchestrating the attack (even if you're sure)
  • Do NOT engage trolls or bot accounts in public replies
  • Do NOT ask admins to attack the competitor's content (creates escalation cycle)
  • Do NOT rush out a defensive statement — take 2-4 hours to craft a measured response
  • Do NOT file a legal threat publicly (WhiteHat Jr lesson — Streisand effect)

What TO Do:

  • Document everything (screenshots with timestamps) for potential future legal action
  • Prepare factual response with verifiable data points
  • Mobilize organic supporter network (admin ambassadors, loyal users) to share positive experiences
  • Brief top admins privately with talking points
  • If a media article was planted, contact the publication with your side of the story
  • Consider proactive positive PR — publish a data report, user milestone, or brand partnership win
