01

Indian Tax Framework for Affiliate Income

▼

Every GrabCash admin earning commissions or cashback must understand how Indian income tax law classifies their earnings. Affiliate income falls under specific heads of income in the Income Tax Act, 1961, and the classification determines everything—from the ITR form you file to the deductions you can claim and the tax rate you pay.

Key Principle

Affiliate commission income is almost always classified as "Income from Business or Profession" under Section 28 of the Income Tax Act, 1961. Only in rare cases where affiliate activity is occasional and non-systematic can it be classified as "Income from Other Sources" under Section 56.

1.1 How Affiliate Income is Classified

The Income Tax Act recognises five heads of income: Salary, House Property, Business/Profession, Capital Gains, and Other Sources. For GrabCash admins, the critical question is whether their affiliate earnings constitute "Business Income" or "Other Sources."

1.2 Key Income Tax Act Provisions for Affiliate Income

Section 28 — Business Income

Any profits and gains of a business or profession carried on by the assessee are chargeable under this head. For GrabCash admins who actively manage affiliate channels, share deals, and earn commissions, this is the applicable provision.

Key advantage: All legitimate business expenses can be deducted from gross income before computing tax.

Section 44AD — Presumptive Taxation

Available to resident individuals, HUFs, and partnership firms (not LLPs) with turnover up to Rs.3 crore (if digital receipts exceed 95% of total, the limit is Rs.3 Cr; otherwise Rs.2 Cr). Deemed profit is 6% of digital turnover or 8% of cash turnover. No need to maintain books of accounts.

GrabCash Advantage: Since all GrabCash payouts are digital (bank transfer/UPI), the effective deemed profit rate is just 6%.

Section 44ADA — Professionals

For professionals with gross receipts up to Rs.75 lakh (Rs.50L if digital receipts are under 95%). Deemed profit is 50% of gross receipts. Applicable to specified professions (legal, medical, engineering, architecture, accountancy, interior decoration, etc.). Affiliate marketing is generally not classified as a profession under this section.

Section 56(2)(x) — Other Sources

Covers income from property, dividends, interest, and gifts exceeding Rs.50,000 in aggregate from non-relatives. Critical for cashback: if total cashback received by an individual exceeds Rs.50,000/year and is classified as a "gift" or "benefit," it becomes taxable under this section.

1.4 Old vs New Regime: Which Should GrabCash Admins Choose?

Old Regime: Best For

  • Admins with heavy business expenses (internet, hosting, phone, travel, office space)
  • Those claiming Section 80C (PPF, ELSS, LIC), 80D (health insurance), HRA, LTA
  • Income between Rs.10L–20L with deductions exceeding Rs.3L
  • Admins with home loans (Section 24 interest deduction up to Rs.2L)

Break-even point: If your total deductions/exemptions exceed Rs.3.75L, Old Regime is typically better.

New Regime: Best For

  • Admins with minimal expenses or using presumptive taxation (44AD)
  • Income up to Rs.12L (zero tax due to rebate)
  • Income above Rs.20L with fewer than Rs.3.75L in deductions
  • Simplicity seekers—no receipts/proofs needed for deductions

Default regime: New Regime is the default from FY 2023-24 onwards. You must explicitly opt out to choose Old Regime.

1.6 Advance Tax Requirements

If your estimated tax liability for the year (after TDS) exceeds Rs.10,000, you must pay advance tax in quarterly installments. Failure to pay results in interest under Sections 234B and 234C.

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) Deep Dive

▼

TDS is the Indian government's mechanism for collecting tax at the point of payment rather than at the end of the year. For GrabCash admins, understanding TDS is critical because it directly affects the amount you receive in your bank account—and how you reconcile it when filing your ITR.

Section 194H
2%
TDS on commission/brokerage exceeding Rs.20,000/year (revised from 5% to 2% from FY 2025-26).
Section 194J
10%
TDS on professional/technical services exceeding Rs.30,000/year.
Section 194-O
1%

2.1 Section 194R — TDS on Benefits/Perquisites

Introduced by Finance Act 2022 (effective July 1, 2022), Section 194R requires any person providing a benefit or perquisite exceeding Rs.20,000 in aggregate during a financial year to deduct TDS at 10% before providing such benefit. This section has direct implications for cashback and incentive programs.

Parameter Details
Applicable to Any person (other than individual/HUF with turnover below Rs.1 Cr business or Rs.50L profession in preceding FY) providing benefit/perquisite to a resident
Threshold Rs.20,000 aggregate value of benefits in a financial year
TDS Rate 10% (20% if PAN not furnished)
What is covered Incentives (other than trade discounts/rebates), free products, sponsored trips, gift vouchers, free event tickets, cashback exceeding trade discount
What is NOT covered Trade discounts/rebates given at the time of sale, benefits already covered under Section 192 (salary perquisites)
GrabCash Impact If GrabCash provides non-commission benefits (bonus rewards, contest prizes, free products for review) exceeding Rs.20K/year to an admin, 194R TDS applies
Critical: Cashback vs Commission Under 194R

Commission payments to admins are covered under Section 194H, not 194R. However, non-monetary incentives, bonus rewards, and benefits in kind fall under 194R. If GrabCash gives an admin a free smartphone worth Rs.25,000 as a performance incentive, TDS of Rs.2,500 (10%) must be deducted. The admin must pay this TDS to GrabCash before receiving the benefit, or GrabCash must gross up the value.

2.4 Section 194-O — E-Commerce Transactions

Section 194-O was introduced to bring the e-commerce ecosystem under TDS. An e-commerce operator facilitating the sale of goods or provision of services is required to deduct TDS at 1% on the gross amount of such sale or service at the time of credit/payment to the e-commerce participant.

Does 194-O Apply to GrabCash?

Analysis: Section 194-O applies to "e-commerce operators" who facilitate the sale of goods/services of an "e-commerce participant." GrabCash is an affiliate/cashback platform, not a marketplace where goods are sold. Admins do not sell goods through GrabCash—they earn commissions for driving traffic. Therefore, 194-O likely does NOT apply to GrabCash-admin relationships. The applicable section is 194H. However, if GrabCash evolves into a marketplace model, 194-O would become applicable. Consult a tax professional for specific advice.

2.6 TDS Certificates & Reconciliation

Form 16A

TDS certificate issued by GrabCash (deductor) to the admin (deductee) for TDS deducted under sections other than salary. GrabCash must issue Form 16A quarterly within 15 days of the TDS return filing due date.

  • Q1 (Apr-Jun): By Aug 15
  • Q2 (Jul-Sep): By Nov 15
  • Q3 (Oct-Dec): By Feb 15
  • Q4 (Jan-Mar): By Jun 15

Form 26AS (Tax Credit Statement)

Your consolidated annual tax credit statement available on the Income Tax e-filing portal. Shows all TDS deducted against your PAN by all deductors (GrabCash, banks, etc.), advance tax paid, self-assessment tax paid, and refunds processed. Always verify your 26AS before filing ITR.

AIS (Annual Information Statement)

A comprehensive statement introduced in 2021 that shows all financial transactions linked to your PAN—including savings account interest, share transactions, property transactions, foreign remittances, and TDS. AIS is more detailed than 26AS and is available on the AIS portal (compliance portal).

2.7 TDS Timeline: Month-by-Month Flow

Day of Payment

GrabCash credits commission to admin's account. TDS is deducted at the time of credit or payment, whichever is earlier. Net amount (gross minus TDS) is transferred to admin's bank.

7th of Next Month

GrabCash deposits the deducted TDS with the government via Challan No. 281. For March, the deadline is April 30.

End of Quarter

GrabCash files quarterly TDS return (Form 26Q) with details of all deductions made during the quarter. Deadlines: Jul 31, Oct 31, Jan 31, May 31.

15 Days After TDS Return Due Date

GrabCash issues Form 16A to the admin. Admin should download and verify against 26AS.

ITR Filing Time

Admin claims TDS credit in ITR. The TDS amount reduces the total tax liability. If TDS exceeds actual tax, the difference is refunded by the IT department (typically within 4-8 weeks of e-verification).

2.8 How to Claim TDS Credit When Filing ITR

1

Download Form 26AS/AIS

Login to incometax.gov.in → My Account → View Form 26AS (redirects to TRACES). Also check AIS on the Compliance Portal for comprehensive data.

2

Verify All TDS Entries

Cross-check every TDS deduction shown in 26AS with your GrabCash payout records and Form 16A. Ensure TAN of deductor, amount, and section are correct.

3

Report Discrepancies

If 26AS shows lower TDS than what was actually deducted, contact GrabCash support. They may need to file a revised TDS return or correction statement.

4

Fill Schedule TDS in ITR

In your ITR form, fill Schedule TDS-1 (salary) and Schedule TDS-2 (non-salary). Enter deductor TAN, name, gross income, and TDS deducted for each entry.

5

Claim Credit & Get Refund

The ITR utility automatically calculates total TDS credit. If TDS exceeds tax liability, the difference is refunded. Ensure your bank account is validated for direct credit of refund.

3.5 Composition Scheme for Small Admins

The Composition Scheme is a simplified GST compliance option for small taxpayers. For service providers, the scheme is available with turnover up to Rs.50 lakh and a flat tax rate of 6% (3% CGST + 3% SGST) on turnover.

Regular GST Scheme

  • GST Rate: 18% on services
  • Input Tax Credit: Available
  • Invoice requirement: Must issue tax invoice with GST
  • Returns: GSTR-1 + GSTR-3B (monthly/quarterly)
  • Inter-state supply: Allowed
  • E-commerce supply: Allowed
  • Best for: Admins with significant ITC claims

Composition Scheme

  • GST Rate: 6% flat on turnover
  • Input Tax Credit: NOT available
  • Invoice requirement: Must issue "Bill of Supply" (not tax invoice)
  • Returns: CMP-08 (quarterly) + GSTR-4 (annual)
  • Inter-state supply: NOT allowed
  • E-commerce supply: NOT allowed (restriction lifted for some categories)
  • Best for: Admins with minimal ITC and turnover <Rs.50L

3.8 E-Invoicing Requirements

E-Invoicing Thresholds (FY 2025-26)

E-invoicing is mandatory for businesses with aggregate turnover exceeding Rs.5 crore in any preceding financial year from FY 2017-18 onwards. For most GrabCash admins, this threshold will not be crossed. However, if you are a large-scale affiliate operation with turnover above Rs.5 Cr, you must generate e-invoices through the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP) and obtain an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) for each B2B invoice.

3.9 Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM) on Foreign Services

If a GrabCash admin receives services from a company located outside India (e.g., SaaS tools like Canva, Buffer, SEMrush; or advertising services from Google/Meta), the admin must pay GST under Reverse Charge Mechanism (Section 9(3) and 9(4) of CGST Act).

How RCM Works for Foreign Services

  • The admin (recipient in India) is liable to pay GST instead of the foreign supplier
  • GST is payable as IGST @ 18% on the value of imported services
  • Payment must be made on a reverse charge basis in GSTR-3B
  • The IGST paid under RCM is available as Input Tax Credit in the same return period
  • This effectively makes the net cost zero (pay IGST, claim ITC), but compliance is mandatory

Cashback & Commission Legal Framework

▼

The legal treatment of cashback in India sits at the intersection of tax law, RBI regulations, and consumer protection. Understanding whether cashback is a "discount," "income," or "gift" is critical for both GrabCash as a platform and admins who earn and distribute it.

7.1 Is Cashback a "Discount" or "Income"?

Cashback as Discount (Non-Taxable)

When cashback is given at the point of sale as an instant reduction in price, it is treated as a trade discount and is not taxable for the recipient. Example: "Buy for Rs.1,000, get Rs.100 instant cashback" = effective price Rs.900.

  • GST is charged on the net amount (Rs.900 in the example)
  • No TDS obligation under Section 194R
  • No income reporting for the buyer
  • This is the cleanest structure from a tax perspective

Cashback as Income (Potentially Taxable)

When cashback is credited post-purchase into a wallet or bank account, it may be treated as income/benefit under certain conditions:

  • Section 56(2)(x): If aggregate benefits from non-relatives exceed Rs.50,000/year, the entire amount becomes taxable as "Income from Other Sources"
  • Section 194R: If aggregate benefits exceed Rs.20,000/year from a single payer in a business context, TDS at 10% applies
  • Note: CBDT Circular 12/2022 clarified that cashback provided to consumers in B2C transactions is generally not subject to 194R (applies only in business/profession context)
Key Distinction for GrabCash Admins

For users/consumers: Cashback received through GrabCash for personal purchases is generally not taxable unless aggregate non-relative benefits exceed Rs.50,000/year. The Rs.50,000 threshold aggregates ALL such receipts from all sources, not just GrabCash.

For admins (business context): Commissions received are always taxable as business income regardless of amount. Any additional benefits/perquisites are subject to Section 194R if they exceed Rs.20,000/year.

9.3 Payment Aggregator License

15.5 FAQ: Top 25 Tax Questions from Affiliate Admins

# Question Answer
1 Is affiliate income taxable? Yes. All affiliate commission income is taxable as business income under Section 28 of the Income Tax Act.
2 Do I need to file ITR if my income is below Rs.2.5L? Filing is not mandatory if total income is below the basic exemption limit. However, it is recommended for TDS refund claims and maintaining financial records.
3 Which ITR form should I use? ITR-4 (Sugam) if using presumptive taxation under 44AD. ITR-3 if maintaining books of accounts with actual profit computation.
4 What is 44AD and how does it help me? Section 44AD allows you to declare only 6% of digital turnover as profit (instead of actual profit). No need to maintain books. Significantly reduces tax liability for most admins.
5 Can I use 44AD if I earn Rs.30L/year? Yes, as long as your total turnover is under Rs.3 Cr (for 95%+ digital receipts) and you are a resident individual, HUF, or partnership firm (not LLP).
6 What if GrabCash already deducted TDS? Do I still need to file ITR? Yes. TDS is an advance tax payment, not the final tax. You must file ITR to claim TDS credit, report total income, and compute final tax liability (or get refund).
7 How do I get a TDS refund? File your ITR correctly with all TDS details. If TDS exceeds your actual tax liability, the excess is automatically refunded to your pre-validated bank account (typically 4-8 weeks after e-verification).
8 Do I need GST registration? Only if your aggregate annual turnover from affiliate services exceeds Rs.20L (Rs.10L for NE/hill states). Below this, GST registration is optional.
9 What is the GST rate on affiliate commissions? 18% (9% CGST + 9% SGST for intra-state, or 18% IGST for inter-state)
10 If I register for GST, do I charge it to GrabCash or pay it myself? You charge GST to GrabCash on your invoice. GrabCash pays the invoice amount including GST. You then deposit the GST to the government through GSTR-3B.
11 Can I claim my internet bill as a business expense? Yes, if using regular computation (ITR-3). Not separately claimable under 44AD (already covered in the 6% deemed expense). GST ITC on internet bill is claimable regardless.
12 Is cashback I receive as a user taxable? Generally no, unless your aggregate non-relative benefits exceed Rs.50,000/year under Section 56(2)(x).
13 Do I need to pay advance tax? Only if your estimated tax liability (after TDS) exceeds Rs.10,000 for the year. Under 44AD, you can pay 100% by March 15 in one installment.
14 What happens if I don't file ITR? Late filing fee of Rs.5,000 (Rs.1,000 if income <Rs.5L), interest at 1%/month on outstanding tax, inability to carry forward losses, and possible prosecution for persistent non-filing.
15 Should I choose Old or New tax regime? New Regime is better for most admins using 44AD (low deemed profit, fewer deductions needed). Old Regime is better if you have heavy deductions (80C, 80D, HRA, home loan interest) exceeding Rs.3.75L.
16 What is Form 26AS and how do I access it? Form 26AS is your annual tax credit statement showing all TDS deducted against your PAN. Access: incometax.gov.in → My Account → View Form 26AS.
17 Can I work with EarnKaro and GrabCash simultaneously? Yes, unless your GrabCash admin agreement has a non-compete clause. Consolidate income from all platforms when filing ITR.
18 Do I need to maintain books of accounts? Not required under 44AD (presumptive taxation). Required under regular computation if income exceeds Rs.2.5L or turnover exceeds Rs.25L (Section 44AA).
19 What if I earn from international affiliates? Report foreign income in Schedule FSI. Obtain FIRC from bank. Claim Foreign Tax Credit (Form 67) if tax deducted abroad. Disclose foreign accounts in Schedule FA.
20 When should I incorporate a company? Consider incorporation when revenue exceeds Rs.50L/year, you plan to raise external funding, or you need enhanced credibility with large merchants.
21 Is there TDS on my cashback earnings as an admin? Commission: Yes, TDS under 194H at 2% (above Rs.20K/year). Additional benefits: TDS under 194R at 10% (above Rs.20K/year).
22 Can I get a tax audit even with 44AD? Yes, if you declare profit lower than 6%/8% and your income exceeds the basic exemption limit. Also if you opted out of 44AD in a previous year and are in the 5-year lock-out period.
23 How much should I set aside for taxes? Under 44AD (New Regime): approximately 1-2% of gross commission (since 6% deemed profit falls below or within low tax slabs). Under regular computation: 15-30% depending on tax bracket.
24 Do I need to disclose my GrabCash earnings to my employer? If you are also employed (salaried), you should report your affiliate income to your employer if they need to adjust TDS. Alternatively, pay advance tax on the additional income yourself.
25 Can I hire a CA through GrabCash? GrabCash may offer CA referral services or tie-ups with tax filing platforms at discounted rates for admins. Check the admin dashboard or reach out to your admin manager.

Real-world compliance situations can be complex and stressful. This section walks through the most common risk scenarios GrabCash admins face, with step-by-step resolution guidance.

Scenario 1: Admin Earns Rs.50L But Has Not Registered for GST

Situation: Priya has been a GrabCash admin for 2 years. Her total commission crossed Rs.50L in FY 2025-26, but she never applied for GST registration. She did not realize the Rs.20L threshold had been crossed in October 2024. She now discovers the issue in March 2026.

Consequences:

  • Late registration penalty: Rs.10,000 under Section 122(1)(xi) of CGST Act (Rs.5,000 CGST + Rs.5,000 SGST) or 10% of tax due, whichever is higher
  • Tax liability: GST at 18% on all turnover from the date she should have registered (when turnover crossed Rs.20L). On Rs.30L of turnover after threshold = Rs.5,40,000 GST liability
  • Interest: 18% per annum on the outstanding GST from the date it was due
  • Loss of ITC: Cannot claim ITC for the period she was unregistered
  • Impact on GrabCash: GrabCash may receive notice for not verifying admin GST status for high-value payouts
Resolution Steps
  1. Immediately: Apply for GST registration on gst.gov.in (processing: 3-7 days)
  2. Within 30 days of registration: File GST returns for all past periods from the effective date of registration
  3. Pay outstanding GST + interest: Calculate 18% on all commission income from the month turnover crossed Rs.20L
  4. Consider voluntary disclosure: Approach GST authorities proactively to reduce penalty exposure
  5. Engage a CA: For proper computation of past liability and representation before authorities
  6. Going forward: Set up automated alerts for turnover milestones in accounting software
Scenario 2: IT Notice for Unreported Affiliate Income

Situation: Vikram receives a notice under Section 148A (notice before assessment/reassessment) from the Income Tax Department. The AIS (Annual Information Statement) shows Rs.8,00,000 in receipts from GrabCash, but Vikram filed ITR-1 showing only salary income. He did not report his affiliate earnings.

Consequences:

  • Reassessment: The IT department will reassess his income including the unreported Rs.8L
  • Additional tax: Tax on Rs.8L at his marginal rate (could be 20-30%)
  • Interest under Section 234A: 1% per month from the original due date
  • Interest under Section 234B: 1% per month for not paying advance tax
  • Penalty under Section 270A: 50% of the under-reported tax (misreporting) or 200% (mis-reporting like fraudulent intent)
  • Wrong ITR form: Filing ITR-1 for business income is itself a deficiency
Resolution Steps
  1. Do NOT ignore the notice. Respond within the deadline specified (usually 15-30 days)
  2. Hire a CA immediately. A professional is essential for IT notice responses
  3. Gather documents: GrabCash payout statements, bank statements, Form 26AS, Form 16A
  4. File a revised return: If within the revision window, file ITR-3 or ITR-4 with the correct income figures and use Section 44AD to minimize tax
  5. Respond on the IT portal: Login to incometax.gov.in → Pending Actions → Response to Notice. Submit reply with all supporting documents
  6. Pay the additional tax + interest: Use Challan 280 to pay before the response deadline to show good faith
  7. Request penalty waiver: Under Section 273B, reasonable cause can be cited for penalty waiver (first-time offense, ignorance, prompt rectification)
Scenario 3: TDS Mismatch Between 26AS and Actual Earnings

Situation: Anita earned Rs.12,00,000 from GrabCash in FY 2025-26. Her bank statements show TDS deductions totaling Rs.24,000 (2% of Rs.12L). However, her Form 26AS only shows Rs.16,000 TDS credit. There is a mismatch of Rs.8,000.

Cause Analysis:

  • GrabCash may have deposited the full TDS but filed the TDS return with incorrect data
  • GrabCash may not have deposited the last quarter's TDS yet (check timing)
  • There could be a PAN mismatch in GrabCash's records
  • The TDS return for the relevant quarter may not have been processed by CPC yet
Resolution Steps
  1. Wait for processing: 26AS data updates after the deductor (GrabCash) files the quarterly TDS return AND it is processed by CPC. Check again after 15 days of the TDS return due date.
  2. Contact GrabCash: Email admin support with your PAN, the specific quarters/months of mismatch, and your Form 16A as evidence. Request them to verify their TDS return data.
  3. GrabCash action: If the error is in GrabCash's TDS return, they must file a correction statement on TRACES (TDS filing portal) to update the data.
  4. File ITR with available credit: If the mismatch cannot be resolved before the ITR due date, file with the TDS credit as shown in 26AS. You can file a revised return later when the full credit appears.
  5. Do NOT claim TDS credit not appearing in 26AS: The IT department's automated system will flag the excess credit and issue a demand notice.
  6. Escalate if needed: If GrabCash does not resolve within 30 days, file a complaint on TRACES and consider approaching the Assessing Officer.
Scenario 4: Admin Operates From Multiple States

Situation: Rajesh is a GrabCash admin registered in Telangana (Hyderabad) but also operates from Maharashtra (Mumbai office) and Karnataka (Bangalore co-working space). He has team members in all three states. His total commission is Rs.40L/year.

GST Implications:

  • GST registration is state-specific. If Rajesh has a "fixed establishment" (office, regular presence) in multiple states, he may need separate GST registrations in each state
  • Invoices must be issued from the state where the service is supplied from
  • Intra-state supply: CGST + SGST (to GrabCash in Hyderabad from Hyderabad office)
  • Inter-state supply: IGST (to GrabCash in Hyderabad from Mumbai/Bangalore)
  • Place of Supply for services: Generally the location of the recipient (GrabCash, Hyderabad) under Section 12 of IGST Act
Resolution Steps
  1. Determine principal place of business: Where do you primarily operate from? Register GST there first.
  2. Assess additional places: If you have a permanent office/establishment in other states, register as "additional place of business" or obtain separate state registrations.
  3. Simplify: If possible, structure all invoices from your principal state. Use co-working spaces as temporary arrangements, not fixed establishments.
  4. Consult a CA: Multi-state GST is complex. Professional advice is essential to avoid incorrect registration or IGST/SGST confusion.
  5. Income Tax: ITR is filed at the central level (not state-specific). Report all income regardless of state of earning.
Scenario 5: Admin Receives Foreign Commissions

Situation: Meera works with GrabCash (domestic commissions of Rs.15L) and also earns $5,000/year from CJ Affiliate and $3,000 from Amazon Associates US. Total foreign income: approximately Rs.6.5L. She receives payments via Payoneer to her Indian bank account.

Compliance Requirements:

  • Income Tax: Foreign affiliate income must be reported in Indian ITR under Schedule FSI (Foreign Source Income). Total income = Rs.15L (GrabCash) + Rs.6.5L (foreign) = Rs.21.5L
  • FEMA: Receiving foreign remittances for services is permitted under FEMA. No prior RBI approval needed for service exports.
  • GST: Export of services to foreign companies is zero-rated. File LUT (Letter of Undertaking) to export without paying GST. No reverse charge if services are exported.
  • FIRC: Obtain Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate from the bank for each remittance above the threshold.
  • Payoneer/PayPal: These intermediaries may not issue FIRC directly. You may need to obtain it from your receiving bank.
  • Schedule FA: If you have a Payoneer/PayPal account that holds foreign currency, it may need to be disclosed as a foreign asset in Schedule FA of ITR.
Resolution Steps
  1. Maintain records: Keep all payment receipts, Payoneer/PayPal transaction history, and exchange rate used for conversion
  2. File LUT annually: Submit Letter of Undertaking on GST portal (Form GST RFD-11) before April 1 of each year to export services without IGST
  3. Claim Foreign Tax Credit: If US/other country deducted withholding tax, claim FTC using Form 67 before ITR due date
  4. Report in ITR: Use Schedule FSI for income details and Schedule FA for foreign accounts (Payoneer, PayPal, etc.)
  5. Keep FIRC/FIRA: Obtain from your bank. If bank doesn't issue automatically, request it for each quarter.
  6. Purpose code: Ensure bank records purpose code P0802 or P0805 for the remittance
Scenario 6: GrabCash Fails to Deduct TDS

Situation: GrabCash paid Rs.5,00,000 commission to admin Sanjay during FY 2025-26 without deducting any TDS under Section 194H. The threshold of Rs.20,000 was clearly crossed. Sanjay files his ITR correctly with the full Rs.5L income, but there is no TDS credit in 26AS.

Legal Position:

  • GrabCash's liability: The deductor (GrabCash) is liable for failure to deduct TDS. Under Section 201(1), GrabCash is treated as an "assessee in default" and must pay the TDS amount + interest at 1%/month from the date TDS was deductible to the date of actual deduction/payment
  • Additional penalty: GrabCash faces penalty under Section 271C equal to the amount of TDS not deducted
  • Admin's position: The admin is NOT penalized for the deductor's failure, provided the admin has reported the income correctly in their ITR and paid tax on it
  • Section 205 protection: The admin cannot be asked to pay the TDS that GrabCash failed to deduct, as long as the income is reported and tax is paid in the ITR
Resolution Steps (For Admin)
  1. Report income correctly: File ITR with the full Rs.5L commission income, regardless of TDS status
  2. Pay full tax: Since no TDS credit is available, the admin must pay the full tax via advance tax / self-assessment tax
  3. Inform GrabCash: Notify GrabCash in writing about the TDS failure. This protects the admin and creates documentation
  4. No TDS credit claim: Do not claim TDS credit that does not appear in 26AS. The system will reject it
  5. If IT department raises demand: Cite Section 205 and provide proof that income was reported and tax paid. The demand should be directed to GrabCash, not the admin
Scenario 7: Admin Gets Audited

Situation: GrabCash admin Kavita earned Rs.1.2 Cr in commissions during FY 2025-26. She was using Section 44AD presumptive taxation. However, she receives a notice for Scrutiny Assessment under Section 143(3). She is selected for detailed audit.

Analysis:

  • 44AD limit: Rs.1.2 Cr is within the Rs.3 Cr limit for digital businesses. So 44AD is valid.
  • But: High-value cases are frequently selected for scrutiny. The IT department may question whether 6% deemed profit is reasonable for a business with Rs.1.2 Cr turnover.
  • Possible queries: Source of business, nature of expenses, consistency of income over years, bank account details, rationality of 6% profit rate, details of business operations
Document Preparation Guide for Audit
  1. Hire a CA (Mandatory): A CA is essential for representing before the Assessing Officer
  2. Prepare the following documents:
    • ITR filed for the year with computation of income
    • All bank statements (every account) for the FY
    • GrabCash commission statements / dashboard screenshots
    • Form 26AS and AIS printouts
    • GST returns filed (if registered)
    • Copy of admin agreement with GrabCash
    • Details of business model (how affiliate marketing works)
    • Invoices issued (if GST registered)
    • Details of expenses incurred (even under 44AD, having records helps)
    • PAN and Aadhaar copies
    • Previous year ITRs (for consistency check)
  3. Respond within timeline: Scrutiny notices specify a hearing date. Attend (in person or through CA) and submit documents as requested. Adjournments can be requested but not abused.
  4. Cooperate fully: Non-cooperation leads to best judgment assessment (ex-parte) where the AO estimates income, often unfavorably
  5. Assessment order: The AO will pass an assessment order (typically within 12-18 months). If unfavorable, appeal to CIT(A) within 30 days.
