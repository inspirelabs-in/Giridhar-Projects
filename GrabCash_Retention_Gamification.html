Cohort Analysis Framework

We recommend tracking retention across four cohort dimensions to isolate what drives long-term engagement. Each dimension reveals different optimization opportunities.

Acquisition Cohorts (by signup week)

Track D1/D7/D30/D90 retention for each weekly signup cohort. This reveals whether product changes or marketing channel shifts impact quality. Compare organic vs. paid vs. referral cohorts to identify highest-LTV sources.

SELECT DATE_TRUNC('week', signup_date) AS cohort_week, COUNT(DISTINCT user_id) AS cohort_size, COUNT(DISTINCT CASE WHEN last_active >= signup_date + 1 THEN user_id END) * 100.0 / COUNT(DISTINCT user_id) AS d1_retention FROM users GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;

Behavioral Cohorts (by first action)

Segment users by what they did first: shared a link, browsed deals, added UPI, invited a friend. Users whose first action was sharing a link retain 2.3x better than those who only browsed. First-action is the strongest retention predictor after acquisition source.

Revenue Cohorts (by earning tier)

Users who earn Rs.100+ in their first week have 4.1x higher D90 retention than those who earn nothing. Revenue cohorts help design minimum viable earning thresholds for onboarding. Target: get every user to Rs.10 within 24 hours.

Engagement Cohorts (by feature usage)

Track which features (link creation, deal browsing, leaderboard viewing, referral inviting, withdrawal) correlate most strongly with retention. Users who engage with 3+ features in week 1 retain at 3.8x vs. single-feature users.

"First Earning" Activation Strategy

The single most important retention metric is time-to-first-earning. Every hour of delay reduces D30 retention by approximately 2.3 percentage points. Our strategy ensures users earn their first Rs.10 within 24 hours of signup.

Guaranteed First Earning Mechanics

  • Welcome Bonus Rs.10: Credit Rs.10 to the user's GrabCash wallet upon completing the onboarding checklist (5 simple tasks). This is a subsidy that costs approximately Rs.52,000/month at current signup rates but dramatically improves activation.
  • 2x Commission First 5 Links: Double the commission rate on the user's first 5 shared links. If normal commission is 4%, they earn 8%. This makes the first earning feel substantial and worth the effort.
  • Self-Purchase Allowance: Allow users to earn on their own purchases for the first 3 transactions. Many affiliate platforms prohibit self-purchase, but allowing it initially removes the friction of needing to find a buyer.
  • Instant Earning Confirmation: Show "Pending Earning" immediately when someone clicks the user's link, even before purchase. This provides instant gratification and keeps users checking the app.

Anti-Gaming & Fraud Rules

Streak Psychology & Loss Aversion

Loss aversion is 2x stronger than gain motivation (Kahneman & Tversky). A user with a 14-day streak experiences the potential loss of that streak as roughly equivalent to losing Rs.200 in earned rewards, even if the actual daily check-in bonus is only 20 points.

Streak Escalation Design

  • Days 1-3: Low investment. Easy to restart. Focus on building initial momentum.
  • Days 4-7: First milestone approaching. Users start feeling investment. Streak freeze option introduced.
  • Days 8-14: Moderate loss aversion kicks in. Streak-at-risk notifications become critical. 8 PM reminder if no login.
  • Days 15-30: Strong psychological investment. Users will actively plan around maintaining their streak. Consider auto-check-in if user opens any GrabCash link (even from WhatsApp).
  • Days 31+: Streak becomes part of identity. Users share streak status on social. "I'm on a {n}-day streak!" becomes a social signal. Streak freeze should be cheap or free at this stage (protecting a power user is worth more than the 200-point cost).

Why Referrals Drive Retention

  • Social accountability: When you refer someone, you feel responsible for helping them succeed. This drives continued engagement from the referrer.
  • Ongoing earning trigger: Each referral's activity generates notifications for the referrer ("Your referral just earned!"), creating new touchpoints.

Pipeline Components

  • Kafka Topics: user-events, earning-events, gamification-events, notification-events. Partitioned by user_id for ordering.
  • Gamification Consumer: Processes events, updates user state (points, levels, badges, streaks), emits gamification-events.

  • Channel Router: Selects best channel based on user preference and message priority. P0 messages go to all opted-in channels.
  • Latency Target: Event to notification delivery < 5 seconds for P0, < 30 seconds for P1, batched for P2.
A/B Testing Infrastructure & Feature Flags

A/B Testing Framework

  • Tool: GrowthBook (open-source) or LaunchDarkly
  • Assignment: Deterministic hashing on user_id + experiment_id. Ensures consistent assignment across sessions.
  • Metrics Pipeline: Events flow to BigQuery for analysis. Statistical significance calculated via Bayesian method.
  • Minimum Sample: 500 users per variant (for 80% power at 5% significance).
  • Guardrail Metrics: Every experiment tracks retention, NPS, and support ticket rate as guardrails.

Feature Flag System

  • Flags: gamification_engine_v2, streak_system, weekly_challenges, team_leaderboard, elite_program
  • Rollout: 5% canary → 25% beta → 50% → 100% over 4 weeks
  • Kill Switch: Any flag can be disabled in < 30 seconds via admin dashboard
  • Segmentation: Flags can target by user tier, city, signup date, device type
  • Logging: All flag evaluations are logged for debugging and analysis

Success Criteria & Go/No-Go Decision Framework

At the end of each 30-day phase, the team evaluates metrics against targets. The following framework determines whether to proceed, iterate, or pivot.

DecisionCriteriaAction
GREEN — Proceed Primary metric hits >80% of target AND no guardrail metric regresses >5% Move to next phase as planned. Allocate full budget for next phase.
YELLOW — Iterate Primary metric hits 50-80% of target OR one guardrail metric regresses 5-10% Extend current phase by 2 weeks. Run additional A/B tests. Diagnose specific bottleneck before proceeding.
RED — Pivot Primary metric hits <50% of target OR guardrail metric regresses >10% Halt feature rollout. Conduct root-cause analysis. Consult with stakeholders on revised approach. May need to simplify or restructure the gamification model.

Guardrail Metrics (Must Not Regress)

  • App Store Rating: Must stay above 4.0 stars. Gamification complexity should not frustrate users.
  • Support Ticket Volume: Less than 15% increase in tickets related to points, levels, or rewards confusion.
  • App Uninstall Rate: Must not increase by more than 5% from notification fatigue or complexity.
  • Session Duration: Must not decrease by more than 10% (indicates frustration or confusion).
  • Earning-per-Active-User: Must not decrease (gamification should enhance, not distract from, core earning behavior).
