Brand Personality — 5 Core Traits

1. Empowering

We believe every person's network has value. GrabCash puts earning power in everyone's hands. We celebrate small wins, because ₹200 from sharing a deal is someone's recharge money. We never talk down to users or assume they need handholding.

We say "You can start earning right now" Not "It's easy even for beginners"

2. Trustworthy

Backed by GrabOn (India's #1 coupon platform), we carry a legacy of trust. We are transparent about commission rates, payout timelines, and how tracking works. No hidden terms, no inflated earning claims, no "up to ₹50,000/month" clickbait.

We say "Real users earned ₹2,500 last month" Not "Earn lakhs from home!"

3. Relatable

We speak the language of our users — Hinglish, memes, real-life scenarios. We understand that our user is sharing deals on WhatsApp family groups, Telegram channels, and Instagram stories. Our tone is like a helpful friend, not a corporate platform.

We say "Woh deal jo tune share ki? Uspe kamao" Not "Monetise your social commerce potential"

4. Energetic

Affiliate earning should feel exciting, not like a side gig. We bring high energy to everything — from push notifications ("Your ₹180 just landed!") to social posts. Celebrations are loud and frequent. Every earning milestone is a party.

We say "Boom! ₹450 earned this week!" Not "Your commission has been processed"

5. Transparent

We show exactly what users earn, how tracking works, when payouts happen, and why some commissions get cancelled. No black-box algorithms, no vague "processing" statuses. If a commission is pending validation by the brand, we say so. If a user's link didn't track, we explain why and help fix it. Radical transparency is our competitive moat against platforms that hide behind dashboards.

We say "Commission pending — brand validates in 30-60 days. Here's why." Not "Under review"

5 Voice Principles

GrabCash's voice is consistent across all channels. Tone flexes by context, but voice never changes. These five principles define how GrabCash sounds — always.

Principle 1: Talk Like a Friend Who Figured It Out First

We are the friend who discovered a hack and is excitedly telling you about it. Not a teacher, not a sales rep, not a corporate brand. A friend. This means we use contractions, we ask questions, we use "you" and "your" more than "we" and "our."

Do This

"Bro, I made ₹800 last week just sharing Amazon links. Same deals you share anyway — but now they pay you. Want in?"

"That Boat earbuds deal you shared? You could've earned ₹45 on it. Next time, use your GrabCash link."

Not This

"GrabCash enables users to monetise their social commerce activities through affiliate link generation and commission tracking."

"Our platform offers competitive commission rates across 500+ partner brands for affiliate marketers."

Principle 2: Show the Money, Always

Every message should connect to earning. We don't talk about "features" — we talk about "how much you can earn with this feature." Numbers are persuasive. Real earnings are proof. Specific amounts beat vague promises.

Do This

"New: Flipkart commissions up to 9%! A ₹5,000 phone = ₹450 in your pocket."

"Sneha from Jaipur earned ₹3,200 last month sharing beauty deals. Her most shared deal? Lakme foundation at ₹399."

Not This

"We've partnered with Flipkart to offer enhanced commission rates for our affiliate network."

"Earn more with our updated commission structure."

Principle 3: Celebrate Every Rupee

A ₹15 earning is worth celebrating. A ₹50 earning is amazing. A ₹500 earning is legendary. We never diminish small earnings. For a college student, ₹200/week is their phone recharge. For a homemaker, ₹1,000/month is their personal spending money. We match the celebration to the user's reality.

Do This

"₹75 earned! That's your chai money for the week sorted."

"Your first ₹10! The journey starts here. Most top earners started exactly like this."

Not This

"You've earned ₹75. Keep trying to reach bigger milestones!"

"Only ₹10 so far? Share more links to increase your earnings."

Principle 4: Respect the Hustle

Our users are not "passive income seekers." They are hustlers, entrepreneurs, and resourceful people finding ways to earn. We respect the effort of finding good deals, crafting convincing messages, building Telegram channels. This is real work, and we honour it.

Do This

"Your Telegram channel is growing — 50 clicks today! Your deal curation game is strong."

"You shared 23 deals this week. That dedication is what separates earners from browsers."

Not This

"Passive income made easy — just share links and watch the money roll in!"

"Zero effort required! Money while you sleep!"

Principle 5: No Jargon, No Gatekeeping

If a homemaker in Lucknow cannot understand our message in 3 seconds, it's too complicated. We avoid affiliate marketing jargon (CPA, EPM, conversion funnel, attribution window). We translate everything into plain language. If we must use a technical term, we explain it immediately.

Do This

"When someone buys through your link, you earn. The brand confirms the purchase (usually 30-60 days), and then you can withdraw."

"Your link didn't track? It happens when someone clears cookies. Try sending the link again."

Not This

"Post-attribution validation typically takes 30-60 days pending advertiser confirmation of the conversion event."

"Cookie expiry caused attribution failure. Regenerate the deep link and ensure click-through attribution."

Hinglish Code-Switching Guidelines

Hinglish (Hindi-English mix) is a core part of GrabCash's voice for social and casual channels. But bad Hinglish is worse than no Hinglish. Follow these rules.

Golden Rules of GrabCash Hinglish

  1. Write in Roman script, not Devanagari. Our audience reads Hindi faster in Roman. "Kamao" not "कमाओ".
  2. Keep English for numbers, brand names, and technical terms. "₹500 earn karo Myntra se" not "paanch sau rupaye kamao myntra se."
  3. Switch at clause boundaries, not mid-word. "Deal share karo aur paisa kamao" not "Deal share-ify karo."
  4. Match the user's natural mixing pattern. Our users say "link share kar" not "sambandh sanjha kar." Use the words they actually use.
  5. Test with a Tier-2 audience. If someone in Jaipur or Patna wouldn't say it naturally, don't write it.

"Yeh deal share karo — ₹85 ka earning milega"

Inclusive Language Guidelines

  • Gender: Use "they/them" in English content. In Hindi, use gender-neutral constructions where possible. When using gendered examples, alternate — don't always default to male names and pronouns. User stories should feature women earners prominently (they're 40%+ of our base).

Crisis Communication Principles

  1. Acknowledge fast. Even before you have a solution, acknowledge the problem. "We know. We're fixing it." is better than silence.
  2. Be honest. Never deflect, minimise, or blame users. If it's our fault, say so. If it's a third-party issue, explain clearly.
  3. Give timelines. "We expect this to be resolved by 6 PM today" is better than "We're working on it."
  4. Communicate on all channels. In-app banner + Telegram announcement + Email for Medium+ crises.
  5. Follow up after resolution. Post-mortem within 48 hours. What happened, why, and what we're doing to prevent it.
  6. Protect user data. Never share individual user data in public crisis communications.
